summary: Presentation on Simple Ideas, Strong Papers - Lessons from a PhD Journey to Top-Tier Venues.
abstract: Getting a paper into a top venue is part craft, part strategy, and part resilience; very little of this is taught explicitly during a PhD. This talk offers an honest and practical account of the labyrinth of research and publication, drawn from three years of PhD research and personal experience publishing at venues including ICML, ECAI, and ECML PKDD. This talk will cover the uncomfortable realities of peer review at scale, why simple and intuitive ideas can be stronger than unnecessarily complex methods, how to tell a compelling research story rather than writing a technical report, and the practical details that mostly get relegated in most “writing a good research paper” talks, from hyperparameter fairness, reproducibility and statistical rigor to handling rejections, learning from early and sometimes devastating reviews, and knowing when to start aiming for top venues.
